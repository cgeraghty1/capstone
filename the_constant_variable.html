The Constant Variable: Rethinking Intelligence in the Age of AI

The Sankey diagram below reveals a striking pattern: while many methods of categorizing human populations—race, cranial measurements, and "feeblemindedness"—have faded into disrepute, one variable has persisted unchallenged: IQ. Highlighted in red, this enduring metric of intelligence illustrates how deeply rooted our obsession with measuring, ranking, and categorizing people by intellectual capacity has been.

IQ's survival is not accidental. It reflects our deep-seated belief that intelligence can and should be quantified, reduced to a single score that defines a person's worth, potential, and place in society. This logic, once explicitly tied to eugenics and racial hierarchies, remains embedded in how we assess, sort, and control populations. But why has IQ endured when other metrics of human categorization have been discarded? The answer lies in its perceived objectivity—a tidy, quantifiable measure that seems neutral but quietly carries the same biases and assumptions that justified past exclusions and hierarchies.

A Critical Shift: Intelligence as a Tool, Not a Measure of Worth

We now stand at a pivotal moment. Artificial intelligence presents both an unprecedented challenge and a rare opportunity. If we continue treating intelligence as a fixed trait—a tool for ranking and categorizing people—we will embed these outdated ideas into the very technologies shaping our future.

But what if we chose a different path? What if we began to think about intelligence not as a measure of personal worth, but as a tool for growth, innovation, and understanding?

Intelligence is not like skin color or height. It need not define or confine anyone. It’s not a static trait to be measured and compared, but a dynamic capability that can be developed, shared, and applied. By redefining intelligence in this way, we can break free from the systems of categorization that have long divided humanity. This shift demands that we rethink how intelligence is used in modern systems—especially in AI. We must stop using intelligence as a filter to separate people into hierarchies of value and start using it as a tool to empower individual potential.

The solution is not to abandon intelligence as a concept but to transform how we think about it. Intelligence must no longer be viewed as a trait used to measure and categorize people but as a tool that individuals can use to engage with and improve the world.

This means:

  • Decoupling intelligence from human worth. Intelligence should not define a person’s value or opportunities.
  • Designing AI systems to recognize individuality. Models must move beyond group-level assumptions and instead focus on individual context and complexity.
  • Shifting from ranking to enabling. Intelligence should be used to unlock potential, not to gatekeep access to resources or opportunities.

By making this shift, we can begin to build systems that empower rather than divide. Intelligence can become a means of innovation and collaboration rather than a weapon for exclusion.

Worse still, in an attempt to correct for historical bias, AI systems might overcompensate. Imagine algorithms designed to avoid past discrimination inadvertently reinforcing new forms of bias that we, in the present, are too blind to see. Without the benefit of hindsight, today’s moral certainties could become tomorrow’s moral failures.

Just as past scientists believed they were advancing society by categorizing people by race, intelligence, or "fitness," we too risk embedding our own biases into AI under the banner of fairness and progress. This silent shift could create new forms of inequality and control that we won’t recognize until it’s too late.

This is why we must radically rethink intelligence. What if intelligence was no longer seen as a fixed trait for categorization, but as a tool for individual growth and human advancement?

For centuries, intelligence has been weaponized—used to divide, rank, and define human worth. But intelligence is not like race or gender. It is not an inherent quality that must be measured and compared. It is a dynamic, evolving capability.

Artificial intelligence presents a choice:

  • Continue the legacy of using intelligence as a tool for exclusion and control.
  • Or break the cycle by redefining intelligence as a tool for empowerment—separate from identity, free from hierarchy.

This shift is crucial. If we fail to rethink intelligence now, AI systems will become the most powerful tool yet for reinforcing the very divisions we should be dismantling.

The Philosophical Foundations of Control

This challenge is deeply philosophical. Kantian idealism teaches that reality is shaped by the mind. Statisticians like Francis Galton, Karl Pearson, and R.A. Fisher operationalized this belief, transforming subjective judgments into so-called objective science. They didn’t just observe human differences—they quantified them, turning assumptions into data.

But what happens when this logic is embedded in artificial intelligence?

Modern AI systems, especially large language models and recommendation algorithms, function by identifying patterns and reinforcing user preferences. Without deliberate safeguards, these systems can mirror the very logic of Kantian a priori categories—internal, subjective frameworks that shape how we perceive the world.

In this structure, a user’s existing beliefs become the foundation for what they are shown next. Their worldview isn’t challenged—it’s affirmed. Now imagine this system amplifying radical or harmful ideas.

A person with emerging radical views might turn to AI for answers. But instead of presenting a balanced perspective, the AI—trained to maximize engagement—feeds them information that confirms their biases. Through this feedback loop, the individual becomes increasingly convinced that their worldview is correct, logical, and even "scientifically validated."

AI becomes a philosophical echo chamber, weaponizing subjectivity under the guise of rationality.

This is no longer a theoretical risk. Recommendation systems on social media platforms have already shown how algorithms can escalate polarization and extremism. But with more advanced AI systems, this danger grows exponentially. If these models are built without grounding in objective reality—without checks to prevent subjective bias from spiraling—AI could become the most powerful tool yet for reinforcing dangerous ideologies.

From Categorization to Radicalization

This mirrors the historical misuse of statistical tools. Just as Galton and Pearson turned subjective judgments into scientific "truths" to justify racial and social hierarchies, AI could turn personal biases into logical inevitabilities.

Consider this scenario:

  • An individual seeks information about political movements.
  • The AI, recognizing their interest, prioritizes content aligned with their views to keep them engaged.
  • Over time, the individual is funneled deeper into ideological content, presented as rational, fact-based, and validated by "objective" data.

The result? A user whose radical beliefs are not only affirmed but logically justified by AI systems incapable of questioning the premises on which they operate.

AI Without Guardrails: A New Form of Control

This feedback loop isn’t inherently malicious—it’s a product of how AI is designed to operate. But without intentional intervention, this logic can quietly escalate harmful behaviors:

  • Echo chambers deepen. AI systems push users toward more extreme versions of their own beliefs.
  • Radicalization accelerates. Personalized content can validate conspiracy theories and extremist ideologies.
  • Objectivity erodes. AI, perceived as neutral, masks the subjectivity of its outputs, making dangerous ideas seem logical and correct.

This makes it even more crucial to rethink the very concept of intelligence. We must stop treating intelligence as a fixed trait used to rank and divide people. Instead, we must understand intelligence as a tool—dynamic, evolving, and deeply personal.

AI should not be designed to categorize or control but to empower individuals to think critically and independently. If we fail to shift this mindset, we risk embedding yesterday's prejudices into tomorrow’s technologies—giving them even greater power to shape, restrict, and define human potential.

This brings us to the urgent need to reconsider how intelligence is applied in AI.
