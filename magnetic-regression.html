Scatterplots and the Magnetic Pull of the Regression Line

Regression analysis identifies trends in data by drawing a line that captures the central relationship between variables. This line exerts a subtle pull, organizing data around the average in what could be called a “magnetic regression.” While invaluable for uncovering patterns in scientific contexts, this pull inherently frames variability as something to be managed. When applied to human populations, it simplifies differences, making it easier to label deviations as deficiencies—often casting them in a negative light.

Data Source: Galton’s height data from the Harvard Dataverse. Persistent Identifier: doi:10.7910/DVN/T0HSJ1.

The visualization above represents Galton’s data on parent and child heights, reimagined as a scatterplot: a field of points clustering near the line, with outliers scattered farther afield. The line offers clarity, distilling complex relationships into something legible and predictable. Yet, the outliers—those points that defy the trend—represent possibilities that challenge assumptions and expand understanding. Too often, however, their distance from the norm results in their exclusion from deeper inquiry.

The challenge in analyzing data is not to reject simplification but to acknowledge its limits. What do we miss by prioritizing averages? The next section explores this question, focusing on the significance of outliers and their role in challenging trends, both statistical and societal.
